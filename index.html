Case study 01 · Paid search performance

Same budget. Two and a half times the leads.

Industry
General & family dentistry, single location
Channel

My role
Paid media strategist on the agency team

  2. 02 The strategy

  4. 04 The results
  5. 05 The takeaway

The challenge

The practice spent a steady amount every month, but each lead was getting expensive, and lots of people were seeing the ads without clicking.

In Q2 2026 the account converted about 6% of clicks. Impressions were high and click-through was low for a local dental practice, a sign we were showing up for searches that weren't really looking for this practice. The budget wasn't going to grow, so any improvement had to come from spending it better.

The strategy

Narrow the reach, sharpen the message, and make sure bidding learns from the right signal.

Before pushing for volume, we fixed what the algorithm was optimizing toward. A page-view action was counting as a primary conversion, which taught bidding to chase visits instead of patients. Once that was cleaned up, the plan was to tighten keyword match types where the search terms showed waste, and to make the ads more useful at the moment someone decides to call.

The execution

  • Conversion goals. Took a page-view action out of primary and made the lead-form submit primary (May 28).
  • Keywords. Restructured the insurance and financing ad groups and the general dental care ad groups: paused weaker phrase-match terms and added tighter, high-intent ones (Jun 30 – Jul 1).
  • Negatives. Kept adding terms to shared negative keyword lists from regular search term reviews.
  • Creative. Added callouts, sitelinks, and other assets to both Search campaigns, and refreshed the responsive search ads.
  • Budget. Rebalanced daily budgets between the new-patient and orthodontic campaigns throughout July, and built an ad-schedule report to guide timing.

    The challenge

    New accounts start with no history, and this one also had messy tracking.

    The practice launched in late May 2026. Early cost per lead was high, and the conversion setup had overlapping call actions and an unclear lead-form goal. That's a problem because Smart Bidding learns from whatever you tell it counts as success.

    The strategy

    Hold the budget steady, clean up the signal, and then let the data steer.

    Instead of reacting to every early swing, we kept monthly spend consistent so the campaigns could stabilize. We prioritized getting measurement right early, so every optimization afterward would rest on trustworthy numbers.

    The execution

    • Steady budget. Monthly spend stayed within a few percent from June through September.
    • Tracking cleanup (Jul 22). Took a duplicate website-call conversion out of primary goals, removed duplicate call actions, and tied the lead-form conversion to the thank-you page.
    • Ongoing optimization. Ad copy, asset, and budget refinements between the general and cosmetic campaigns.

      Case study 03 · Conversion tracking

      Teaching the algorithm what a lead is.

      Industry
      Dental, 4+ independent practices
      Focus
      Conversion actions, call-quality thresholds, goal settings
      Window
      May 28 – Jul 22, 2026
      My role
      Part of the agency team running the audit
      1. 01 The challenge
      2. 02 The strategy
      3. 03 The execution
      4. 04 The results
      5. 05 The takeaway

      The challenge

      Some accounts were reporting more conversions than clicks. That doesn't mean the campaigns were amazing. It means the tracking was wrong.

      Low-intent actions like directions clicks, menu views, page views, and very short phone calls were set as primary conversions. That inflated reports and, worse, trained Smart Bidding to go after engagement instead of people who actually wanted an appointment.

      The strategy

      Define one standard for what counts as a lead, then apply it account by account.

      Directions clicksMenu viewsPage viewsDuplicate call actionsCalls under the time threshold
      Qualified calls from adsForm submissionsThank-you-page confirmations

      The execution · documented in change history

      1. May 28
        Practice A

        Page-view conversion taken out of primary; lead-form submit made primary.

      2. Jun 8
        Practice C

        Minimum call length for "calls from ads" raised from 15 to 30 seconds.

      3. Jun 8
        Practice D

        Directions taken out of primary; call threshold set to 45 seconds.

      4. Jun 18
        Practice C

        Call threshold raised again, from 30 to 45 seconds.

      5. Jul 9–13
        Practice C

        Directions taken out of primary; thank-you-page conversion revalued.

      6. Jul 22
        Practices B & D

        Duplicate call conversions removed or moved to secondary, a 60-second threshold on website calls, menu views removed, and the lead form renamed to its thank-you page.

      The results

      Reported conversions now reflect real lead actions, not engagement.

      This is a measurement improvement, not a performance gain. Fewer reported conversions after a cleanup like this doesn't mean worse results. It means the numbers can finally be trusted, and every optimization in Cases 01 and 02 depends on that.

      Why there's no "before vs. after" chart here. Raw conversion counts from before and after a tracking change aren't comparable, so charting them side by side would be misleading. The timeline above is the evidence.

      The takeaway

      Every optimization depends on what you've told the platform to optimize for. Getting that right is part of the strategy.
